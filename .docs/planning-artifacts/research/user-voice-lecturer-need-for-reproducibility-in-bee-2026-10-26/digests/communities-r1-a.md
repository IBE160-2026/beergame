# communities-r1-a: lecturer/student voice on demand patterns, grading, comparability

Accessed: 2026-10-26. Angle (a): communities and public posts. Questions owned: Q1 jobs-to-be-done and grading/fairness; Q2 fixed vs random demand complaints; Q3 multi-section comparability; Q4 vocabulary.

Searches run (7, standard mode): "beer game grading teams instructor"; "beer game demand pattern randomized vs fixed"; "reddit beer game professor class experience"; "beer game students anticipate step demand teaching blog"; "beer game syllabus grade participation not based on cost"; "beer game online simulation instructor review multiple sections". (The query set also included one phrase variant of the syllabus search; no identical repeats.)
Fetches (9): HBS Working Knowledge; Zensimu case-study page; Zensimu help-center article; Times Higher Education blog; Jackson & Taylor 1998 PDF; Kaminsky & Simchi-Levi PDF; MERLOT review; Calgary DSI 2010 record. About 15 tool calls total.
Distinct sources actually read: 8 (HBS, Zensimu case page, Zensimu help article, THE blog, Jackson&Taylor, Kaminsky&Simchi-Levi, MERLOT, Calgary DSI abstract).

HEADLINE: public data is thin. No Reddit, HN, Stack Exchange, LinkedIn or Medium threads surfaced (search engine returned none; the search tool is US-only/standard and not social-optimised). Only ONE post from the last 18 months directly addresses fixed-vs-variable demand. No source states that lecturers need identical demand across teams for fair grading. Treat everything below as leads, not prevalence.

## Vocabulary observed (Q4)
"round", "period", "week" (game time unit); "debrief"/"debriefing" (dominant, appears in every source); "bullwhip effect"; "winner" = lowest total cost; "backorder", "stockout"; "compare with other teams"; "training weeks"; "game" vs "session" (Zensimu). Not observed in any source: "replay", "reproducible", "comparable", "fair" in the sense of cross-team/cross-section grading. ("fair comparison" appears once, about centralised vs decentralised cost parameters.)

## Prevalence
No prevalence claim supportable. Fixed-demand-is-predictable complaint: one fresh voice (THE 2026) plus one old academic paper (circa 1998). That is two sources but not two independent contemporary communities. Mark as LEAD / low confidence.
