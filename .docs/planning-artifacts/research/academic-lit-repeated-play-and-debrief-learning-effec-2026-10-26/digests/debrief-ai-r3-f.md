## Findings

1. Kestin et al.: RCT, crossover; 194 eligible of 233 enrolled; post-test n = 142 (AI) vs 174 (in-class); control = in-class active learning (peer instruction in groups of 2-3, then instructor feedback, same content/worksheet); AI tutor custom-built ("PS2 Pal"), structured sequential lesson. Effect: linear regression ES 0.63 (authors call it an underestimate, ceiling effects); quantile regression "an effect size in the range of 0.73 to 1.3 standard deviations"; median post-test 4.5 (AI) vs 3.5 (in-class); median learning gains "over double". Time on task: AI median 49 min vs assumed 60 min in class; time on task not correlated with post-test. Cohen's d not reported as such.
| Kestin G, Miller K, Klales A, Milbourne T, Ponti G. "AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning: an RCT introducing a novel research-based design in an authentic educational setting." https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/ (DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6) | Scientific Reports 15, art. 17458 | 2025 (pub. 3 Jun 2025) | peer-reviewed (Europe PMC: Randomized Controlled Trial; Journal Article) | crossover RCT, post-test n=142/174, ES 0.63 (linear) to 0.73-1.3 SD (quantile) | accessed 2026-10-26 | confidence: high on design/n/ES (PMC full text via fetch), medium on exact wording | class: AI tutoring, NOT debrief; one university course, short single-lesson outcome, custom-engineered pedagogy (not generic LLM). Does not support "AI debrief of a simulation".
   Caveat: single institution (Harvard physics, per brief; PMC summary did not state the institution, unverified in this run). Immediate post-test only, no delayed retention.

2. Bastani et al.: field experiment, "nearly 1,000" high school math students (n from Europe PMC record summary, not exact; exact n NOT verified). Conditions: GPT Base (ChatGPT-like) vs GPT Tutor (prompts with learning safeguards) vs control (no access). During practice: GPT Base +48% grades, GPT Tutor +127%. Access removed: GPT Base students scored 17% lower than never-had-access students; GPT Tutor "largely" mitigated the harm. Quote: "unfettered access to GPT-4 can harm educational outcomes." Authors' mechanism: students used GPT-4 as a "crutch".
| Bastani H, Bastani O, Sungu A, Ge H, Kabakci O, Mariman R.
