### C. Feedback type in dynamic decision tasks
8. Atkins PWB, Wood RE, Rutgers PJ (2002), "The effects of feedback format on dynamic decision making", Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes 88(2):587-604. Graphical feedback gave better performance on a dynamic inventory control task, tabular feedback showed stronger evidence of learning; response times equal; participants recognised complexity factors but could not cope under either format. Sample size and effect sizes not obtained. | https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/the-effects-of-feedback-format-on-dynamic-decision-making/ (abstract page via search summary) | OBHDP | 2002 | peer-reviewed | lab experiment, n unknown | 2026-10-26 | medium (abstract only) | empirical (abstract-level). Note: this compares feedback FORMAT (graph vs table of outcome data), not outcome vs explanatory feedback. Performance and learning dissociate, a design warning for any metric used to judge a debrief.
9. Sengupta K, Abdel-Hamid TK (1993), "Alternative conceptions of feedback in dynamic decision environments: an experimental investigation", Management Science 39(4):411-428, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.39.4.411. Lab experiment, subjects managed simulated software projects; cognitive feedback > feedforward > outcome feedback in performance; outcome feedback had not reliably improved performance in prior work. n and ES not obtained. | https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v39y1993i4p411-428.html ; https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/mnsc.39.4.411 | Management Science | 1993 | peer-reviewed | lab experiment | 2026-10-26 | medium (abstract-level; PDF not opened) | empirical (abstract-level). Direct support that explanation of structure (cognitive feedback) beats raw outcome feedback in a system-dynamics-style task, which is what a debrief supplies over replay alone. One-lab result, no replication check done.
10. Diehl & Sterman (1995) not searched. Related hits seen only as RePEc listings in search results (ideas.repec.org/p/mit/sloanp/2777.html and 2653.html, and a 1999 System Dynamics proceedings paper "Exploration of Outcome Feedback", https://proceedings.systemdynamics.org/1999/PAPERS/PARA98.PDF); content unread. | snippet-only | confidence low.

### D. AI/LLM debrief or feedback with learning outcomes (2024-2026)
11. Kestin G, Miller K, Klales A, Milbourne T, Ponti G (2025), "AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning: an RCT...", Scientific Reports 15:17458, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6, PMC12179260 (full text read, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/). Randomised crossover, Harvard intro physics, 194 analysed of 233 enrolled; AI tutor (GPT-4 with pre-written step solutions) vs in-class active learning, one lesson each in consecutive weeks. Effect 0.63 SD linear regression (authors say underestimated, ceiling effect); quantile regression ~0.73-1.3 SD; median post-test 4.5 vs 3.5; median time on task 49 min vs 60 assumed. Risks stated: LLM hallucination mitigated by pre-written solutions rather than free generation; system prompt alone did not keep multi-part problems in order; single course/single site; ceiling effect; authors overlap with the control-arm instructors; engagement self-reported; risk of tutor used as shortcut on graded work. Earlier preprint (Research Square 2024, DOI 10.21203/rs.3.rs-4243877/v1) claimed "more than twice as much"; the published version does not. | Sci Reports | 2025 | peer-reviewed (preprint earlier) | RCT crossover n=194 | 2026-10-26 | high for what the paper says, medium for generalisation; no replication checked; it is a tutor, not a debrief | empirical. Age: 2025 (over 6 months old, flag).
12. Alnazawi A, Almarhabi M (2026), "Artificial intelligence to support debriefing in simulation-based healthcare education: a scoping review", Advances in Simulation 11(1), DOI 10.1186/s41077-026-00447-6. Arksey and O'Malley / JBI / PRISMA-ScR; MEDLINE, Scopus, WoS, CINAHL; 7 studies (2023-2026; US, Switzerland, Chile, South Korea). Three AI uses: speech/NLP performance analytics; generative AI for facilitator feedback and structured reports; learner-facing reflective-dialogue systems. AI mainly an adjunct to human facilitators. Outcomes mostly feasibility, usability, technical accuracy, perceived value; objective learning improvements limited; evidence limited, exploratory, largely single-institution. | https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID%3A42351276 (record; PMID 42351276) | Adv Simul | 2026 | peer-reviewed | scoping review, k=7 | 2026-10-26 | medium (abstract only); fresh | survey. Directly answers: no controlled learning-outcome evidence for AI debrief in simulation as of this review.
13. Stocks L, et al. (2026), "AI integration in healthcare simulation debriefing: support or substitute?", Advances in Simulation 11(1), DOI 10.1186/s41077-026-00448-5 (PMID 42271527). Explanatory/conceptual, no n. Four modes (metric-based AI tutors, LLM-assisted debriefing tools, conversational chatbot debriefers, hybrid); empirical base "still evolving"; main risk named: over-reliance without human oversight. | Europe PMC record | Adv Simul | 2026 | peer-reviewed | none (conceptual) | 2026-10-26 | medium | theory

Almalki M (2026), "AI-Based Adaptive Simulation Integrated With a Workforce Decision-Support System to Improve Simulated Nursing Leadership Performance: A Randomized Controlled Study", Cureus 18(10), DOI 10.7759/cureus.117315. ### E. Debrief x repetition, dose-response
18. No study crossing debrief with number of plays was found in the sources reached. Closest: Wouters 2013 (multiple sessions and supplementary instruction as separate moderators, #5), Sitzmann 2011 (replay and supplement as separate moderators, #2), Vermylen 2026 (mastery/criterion-based repetition, #6). Not established: spacing/deliberate-practice dose-response with fixed debrief.
