## A. Evidence on repeated play, learning curves, debrief

- Jeong & Hong, "Learning from online beer distribution simulation game", Int. J. Information and Operations Management Education 4(2):179-192. Search snippet (Inderscience abstract page 403 to WebFetch; publisher DOI page https://www.inderscienceonline.com/doi/abs/10.1504/IJIOME.2011.042668 not readable). Secondary summary of the abstract reports: learning increased across repeated plays of the online game; communication level had a significant effect, demand pattern did not; analysis used a regression-based total-cost adjustment. Sample size, number of repeats and design NOT obtained. Repetition effect is NOT separated from debrief (no debrief arm reported in what I could see) | https://www.inderscience.com/filter.php?aid=42668 (via search, not fetched) | Inderscience | 2011 | accessed 2026-10-26 | low (secondary summary of abstract) | evidence
- Martin, Gonzalez & Lebiere, "Learning to Make Decisions in Dynamic Environments: ACT-R Plays the Beer Game" (CMU tech report, 2004). Per search-engine summary only (CMU PDF returned 404, KiltHub 403): it presents empirical evidence that people DO learn in the beer game across trials, contrary to Sterman 1989's misperception claim, plus an ACT-R instance-based model. Participant n, trial count, and any feedback manipulation NOT obtained | https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/journal_contribution/Learning_to_Make_Decisions_in_Dynamic_Environments_ACT-R_Plays_the_Beer_Game/6571235 | Carnegie Mellon University | 2004 | accessed 2026-10-26 | low (not fetched; secondary) | evidence
- Sterman 1989, Management Science (abstract via Crossref): subjects manage a simulated inventory distribution system with multiple actors, feedback, nonlinearities and delays; aggregate dynamics diverge systematically from optimal; an anchoring-and-adjustment stock-management heuristic fits subject behaviour in econometric tests; "misperceptions of feedback" account for poor performance, subjects are insensitive to feedback from their decisions. Sample size not in abstract | https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1287/mnsc.35.3.321 | INFORMS | 1989 | accessed 2026-10-26 | med (abstract only) | evidence
- Croson & Donohue 2006, Management Science (abstract via Crossref/OpenAlex): two experiments on two participant sets in a simple serial supply chain with lags and stochastic demand. Bullwhip persists with operational causes removed; participants consistently underweight the supply line; sharing inventory information does NOT remove the bullwhip or the underweighting but helps somewhat, mainly upstream members. n not in abstract. Treats information sharing, not debrief or repetition | https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1287/mnsc.1050.0436 | INFORMS | 2006 | accessed 2026-10-26 | med (abstract only) | evidence
- Panić, Kovačević, Vujošević, Kuzmanović, "The Impact of Cooperativeness on Supply Chain Performances", Int. J. Engineering Education 36(6):1877-1888. Four groups of engineering students, random assignment to chain with or without information sharing, run twice (n=20 and n=22, total 42); SVO cooperativeness measured before and after play; cooperative teams that could share information had lower costs; information-sharers' cooperativeness scores rose, non-sharers' cooperative participants' scores fell. The "twice" is two cohorts, not repeated play by the same people. Does not test repetition or debrief | https://rfos.fon.bg.ac.rs/handle/123456789/2136?mode=full | Univ. Belgrade FON repository (record of Tempus Publications paper) | 2020 | accessed 2026-10-26 | med (repository abstract summary; full text not read) | evidence
- Systems 8(4):37, "Results of Beer Game Trials Played by Natural Resource Managers Versus Students": 10-year database, over 270 individual players (Texas A&M-Kingsville ranch managers and SDSU honors students); both groups performed worse than players in a seminal study; younger players adapted more readily to inventory surpluses (p<0.10 retailer/distributor, p<0.05 wholesaler/factory). Single-play comparison by age; no repetition or debrief contrast | https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.3390/systems8040037 | MDPI | 2020 | accessed 2026-10-26 | med (abstract only) | evidence
- Cao (Univ. Wisconsin-Superior) poster, "Applying Beer Game in Teaching Bullwhip Effect: Game Board vs. Online", WTFS 2016-17: teams of 4 roles; 4 teams online-class (about 16 students, inferred), 4 teams game-board class, 2 teams volunteered to play BOTH board and online ("Hybrid"). Five open questions scored 0-2 plus three experience items; Hybrid scored highest on all eight items (e.g. Q2 1.75 vs 0.78 board vs 0.69 online), online-only lowest. Hybrid = self-selected volunteers, about 8 students, no stats, so play-twice effect is confounded with selection and with format; no debrief arm | https://www.wisconsin.edu/opid/download/wtfs_reports/wtfs_16-17/Cao-poster-WTFS-2016-17.pdf | University of Wisconsin System | 2017 (report year 2016-17) | accessed 2026-10-26 | low-med (poster, tiny n, self-selected) | evidence
- Gamification in SCM education, Beer Game, Caderno Pedagógico: n=38 students (Logistics technology and Business Administration); results revealed order imbalances and difficulty with unfulfilled orders; students rated game as significant for logical reasoning and concept assimilation; 25-30% had difficulty understanding certain game concepts; authors suggest future sessions add strategies to mitigate the bullwhip and deeper decision analysis. Single intervention, no repetition/debrief contrast | https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.54033/cadpedv21n2-104 | Studies Publicações (Brazil) | 2024 | accessed 2026-10-26 | low-med (abstract only, low-prestige venue) | evidence

Supports a gap the AI debrief could fill; it is an assessment by the authors, not an experiment | https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1002/sdr.1767 (abstract via OpenAlex) | Wiley / System Dynamics Society | 2024 | accessed 2026-10-26 | med (abstract only) | evidence / need

### Does any retrieved study separate repetition from debrief?
NOT FOUND. Nothing retrieved isolates the repetition effect from debrief, and no study found tests an AI/automated debrief. Closest items: Jeong & Hong 2011 (learning across repeated plays, details unretrieved), Martin et al. 2004 (learning across trials, details unretrieved), Cao poster (play-twice, confounded). Croson & Donohue and Panić test information sharing during play, not post-play explanation.
