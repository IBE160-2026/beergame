## (b) Human-likeness of LLM beer-game agents
- Long, Simchi-Levi et al.: in best config (Qwen-3 4B + optimized prompts + data sharing + guardrails) agents cost up to 80% less than human teams; out of the box Qwen-3 4B 77% below, GPT-5 mini 33% below; older models out of the box ~2x human cost, 32% better than humans with orchestrated info. Human benchmark = 12 Georgia Tech cohorts, >100 students | https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17036v4 | arXiv | submitted 2026-05-16, v4 2026-09-21 | accessed 2026-10-26 | high (primary) | paper
- Same paper: "agent bullwhip" = run-to-run instability, erratic orders, occasional very costly outcomes, strongest at factory/distributor, minor at retailer; shown even with fixed demand. Models Phi-4 and DeepSeek-R1-0528 violated basic ordering rules in >25% of cases. Repeated sampling/majority voting (10 or 100 votes) did NOT meaningfully cut variability. No numeric bullwhip ratios in the visible text | https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17036v4 | arXiv | 2026-09-21 | accessed 2026-10-26 | high for as-reported; first 100k of 190k chars read | paper
- Implication for product (my inference, not a paper claim): LLM agents optimize better than humans and are erratic, so they are NOT a faithful stand-in for human-biased ordering. The pedagogical bullwhip may need scripted/biased personas rather than raw LLM | inference from above | n/a | n/a | accessed 2026-10-26 | low | sentiment
- Zhang, Zhang, Zhao "Predicting Effects, Missing Distributions": across 9 behavioral-OM experiments LLMs often reproduce hypothesis-level effects (direction of biases) but response distributions diverge from human data ("dispersion mismatch"), even for strong proprietary models; chain-of-thought and tuning reduce the gap; small/open models can match big ones after tuning. Abstract does not mention bullwhip (a search snippet says it includes a beer game with/without inventory info sharing, 11 groups x 4 roles x 48 weeks, but I did not verify that) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03310 | arXiv | 2025-09-30 (v2 2026-06-27) | accessed 2026-10-26 | high (abstract), low (beer-game detail) | paper
- Jiang et al. "Dynamics of Cognitive Heterogeneity": DeepSeek and GPT agents with different reasoning levels per tier; agents act myopically and self-interestedly, worsening systemic inefficiency; information sharing mitigates; authors say LLMs are promising but limited proxies for humans. No human-likeness measurement in abstract | https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17220 | arXiv | 2026-04-19 (v2 2026-06-02) | accessed 2026-10-26 | med (abstract only) | paper
- AIM-Bench: LLMs show decision biases "resembling" human ones (framing, pull-to-centre) in inventory replenishment; bullwhip addressed via information sharing; no quantitative results visible; not beer game by name | https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11416 | arXiv | 2025-08-15 | accessed 2026-10-26 | med (abstract; >12 months old, stale-ish) | paper

## (c) Cost, latency, reliability
- SupplyNet Appendix F: "a typical simulation session processing 100k to 200k tokens costs approximately $0.25 to $2.00" with gpt-4o; no latency, tokens/round or calls/round reported; smaller local LLMs cut cost but "quality ... and simulation stability may be degraded" | https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24694 | arXiv | 2026-06-23 | accessed 2026-10-26 | high (author-stated, one config) | cost
- Long et al. Table 1 (25-week game, 30 runs, default): GPT-5 mini mean cost 3,927, max 8,644, SD 1,772, CV 45%; Llama 4 Maverick 17B mean 4,026, max 8,912, CV 52%; Qwen-3 4B mean 1,585, max 2,847, CV 26% (cost = game cost units, not dollars). Older models CV 13-46% | https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17036v4 | arXiv | 2026-09-21 | accessed 2026-10-26 | high | cost
- Same: budget-constraint guardrail cut total cost 25% (GPT-5 mini), 39% (GPT-4o mini), 41% (Llama 4 Maverick); spend cap at 2x total customer demand cut Qwen-3 4B mean cost 22%, CV 26% -> 12%, max cost -45%. GRPO post-training of Qwen-3 4B: mean 1,585 -> 952, CV 26% -> 13%, max 2,847 -> 1,353; max order stayed <100 | https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17036v4 | arXiv | 2026-09-21 | accessed 2026-10-26 | high | paper
- Same: no dollars/tokens/latency per game reported; 25-week game x 30 replications per config used as protocol (so a human-scale game is cheap, but this paper gives no price) | https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17036v4 | arXiv | 2026-09-21 | accessed 2026-10-26 | med (appendices unread) | cost
- Prompt/info design: reframing objective cut cost 44% (GPT-4o mini) and 33% (GPT-4.1 mini), negligible on advanced models; demand history + analysis cut GPT-4o mini cost 69% but made advanced models worse | https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17036v4 | arXiv | 2026-09-21 | accessed 2026-10-26 | high | paper
- HBR article PDF as linked from lab page (unreadable binary); outline only: model capability matters more than orchestration; order caps help reliability; selective data sharing; prompt tuning helps weaker models | https://infotheorylab.github.io/beer-game/assets/GenAI_Final_Version_w_Plots.pdf | Information Theory Lab (HBR text, Dec 2025) | 2025-12 | accessed 2026-10-26 | low-med (outline only; file saved locally by tool) | sentiment
